Plenary plan (30 minutes)

Work by type of reasoning, not by suspect, and keep the answer back until step 5. Ask students throughout to name their moves: was it a guess at an explanation, a consequence of one, or a pattern generalised from?

  1. Verdicts, without the answer (3 min). Show the verdicts and confidence below. Say nothing about who was right. Note whether confidence differs between verdicts.
  2. Abduction (7 min). Show the surprising fact. Collect first hypotheses from two or three teams. What made them attractive? Did they name a mechanism, or only a motive? Find an example of a rescue (an accomplice, an affair) and ask what evidence the addition needed. Use the answers to "More than one explanation".
  3. Deduction (7 min). Take one hypothesis and derive predictions together on the board ("if H, then..."). Then Marianne's clean glass: what follows strictly (after she poured, not after she left), and which premises does that rest on? Use "Valid is not the same as sound". Point to the teams that made predictions telling hypotheses apart.
  4. Induction and the planted card (6 min). How much can 30 nights of routine support a claim about one night? Where did teams rely on a pattern without noticing? Then the planted card as a smoking gun that could be planted: contrast a team that took it as confirmation with one that asked what else would have to be true.
  5. Reveal as best explanation (3 min). Present Jonas as the explanation that accounts for most evidence with the fewest additions, and say what remains unproven. If a team argued well for someone else and stated its uncertainty honestly, show it and explain why it scores well.
  6. Transfer (4 min). Map the cycle onto their projects: puzzle, mechanism, observable implications, what their data can be generalised to. Each student writes one sentence: "If my explanation is right, I should find ___ in my data." Collect a few aloud.

Solution and teaching notes

Jonas Brink poisoned the decanter at about 22:48 with tablets he took from Viktor's prescription when he collected it on 14 September. Motive. Viktor's unsent email (E10) announces a review of grant reporting and says Jonas had not produced the partner statements requested on 8 September, so Jonas had reason to fear exposure well before the 14th. Selma reports he had "found out where the money went" (interview). Jonas cancelled a meeting with the external auditor (E9). Means. Forty tablets are missing (E5), enough for the lethal concentration in the small decanter (E1). Jonas collected the prescription (E9, and he admits it). Opportunity. The spare card opened the study at 22:48 (E4) while Viktor was on the terrace with Selma (interview, E11). Apart from one entry on Thursday at 14:12, the spare card had not been used since May (E8). Peter believes Viktor gave it to Jonas on Thursday for the folders (interview, unobserved), which the Thursday entry fits. Jonas's remark that "Viktor kept it himself" suggests he knew Viktor had taken it from the estate office. The bartender puts Jonas out of the bar from about 22:45 to just before 23:00 (E6). Planting. The only person at the coats after 22:50 was Jonas, at 22:55 (E13). His scarf story is weak. Why not Marianne. Her 22:34 entry fits a routine seen on 26 of 30 nights (E8). She has her own card and no need for the spare. But note: her clean glass (E3) only shows the decanter was safe when she poured, not when she left. She could still have poisoned it. The 22:48 entry and the planted card are what count against that. Why not Peter. His false log entry (E7) is explained by the cognac swap. He entered at 22:31, before Marianne poured a clean glass. Why not Selma. She was with Viktor on the terrace from about 22:40 to 23:00 and has no card for the study. What remains uncertain. Nobody saw Jonas enter the study. The card handover is Peter's belief. The case is an inference to the best explanation, not a proof.

Discussion prompts

Abduction. Which explanation did you reach for first, and why was it attractive? What made you give it up, or keep it?

Deduction. What exactly follows from Marianne's clean glass? Which premises did the conclusion depend on?

Induction. How much weight can 30 nights of logs carry? When would a routine be a poor guide to one night?

The planted card. Which teams changed their minds when it appeared, and which asked what else would have to be true?

Your own project. What is your surprising fact? What would follow if your explanation holds? Which data could show you are wrong?

Assessment rubric (0 to 2 points each)

1. The explanation accounts for the surprising fact. 2. Predictions follow from the explanation and could have failed. 3. Evidence is tested against predictions, and the limits of inductive steps are acknowledged. 4. At least one rival explanation is considered, with evidence that discriminates between them. 5. Uncertainty is stated honestly and confidence matches the evidence. Naming the right person earns no points by itself.
